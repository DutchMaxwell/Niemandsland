# Parity FP6 — why the f64-mirror fix (#776) moves the harness flags instead of clearing them

Date: 2026-09-08. Instrument: `tools/position_parity.py` (304 positions, 1702
models) against the pinned baseline `test/fixtures/position_parity/baseline.json`
(source bef020cf, PR 7). Runs on the dedicated parity box, one at a time, three
identical Godot runs each.

## The question

main flags six records against the pin (006 026 028 033 037 128). #776 (B2 cap
circle + f64 mirror, b8593e40) flags seven (003 026 037 109 110 119 128): four
new, three gone. A fix that moves flags instead of clearing them is either
changing geometry or is being measured by an instrument whose tolerance sits
below its own noise. This note measures which, per model.

## The instrument's rule, as it stood

`regressions()` failed a case when any accepted model's distance to the table
exceeded its pinned distance by more than `EPS_IN = 1e-9` inch, or when a model
pinned bit-equal (tier 0) was no longer bit-equal. 1e-9 inch is 2.5e-11 m. A
`Vector3` coordinate on a 72 x 48 inch table is a float32 between 0.25 and 1 m,
whose unit in the last place is 3e-8 to 6e-8 m: **one flipped bit is 1,000 to
2,300 times the tolerance.** Every endpoint crosses three grids on its way to
the comparison — the table's float32 world metre, the planner's f64 inch, and
the float32 inch the planner hands back — so two implementations that both keep
the table's frame and differ only in which grid a moved centre is narrowed to
(main: `from_world_f32`, two float32 roundings; #776: the f64 preimage
`w / IN2M + board / 2`, one narrowing at the output) must produce endpoints a
few units in the last place apart. The pin rule read each such bit as a
regression. That is the whole story for seven of the ten records; the other
three are geometric and stay red on purpose.

### Reading

- **The four new flags (003 109 110 119) are float noise.** Each is one model,
  its excess over the pin 0.07–0.34 ULP (5e-7 to 2.6e-6 in), its endpoint
  0.3–0.5 ULP from main's. Nothing about the push order or the cap circle
  reaches these records; the mirror's single narrowing lands them one grid step
  on the other side of the pinned value. #776 changes no geometry there.
- **The three flags that disappeared (006 028 033) were float noise on main**,
  0.06–0.38 ULP over the pin; #776's f64 mirror lands them exactly on the pinned
  value (excess +0.00), as it does three of the six flagged models of 026.
- **026 is geometric on both** (u01:5 +30,874 ULP = +0.236 in, u01:0 +0.035 in),
  bit-for-bit the same on main and #776 within 0.3 ULP: B1's uncapped charge
  push on an oval base, #799's business, not touched by B2.
- **037 is geometric on both** (+0.004 to +0.027 in on all 21 models, main and
  #776 alike): the collapse-ladder rung the table rejects and the core accepts
  (recorded-037 is 0.12–0.74 in off upstream of the gate), so every last-place
  bit in the push moves its shorten by hundredths. #776 moves four of its
  models (u03:15 to below the pin, u03:16 up by 0.0083 in, u03:0/19 by 2–3
  ULP); parity-frame 5 (#802) owns the rung.
- **128 is the one record where #776 changes geometry, and it is the fix.** The
  world-frame cap circle moves all 21 models 0.027–0.030 in TOWARD the table
  (excess +3,230…+4,056 ULP on main → +57…+72 ULP = +0.00043…+0.00055 in on
  #776). It still sits above the pin by 72 ULP, so it stays red under any
  ULP-scale slack: the remaining 0.0005 in is the whole-unit shorten amplifying
  a residue the gate no longer produces on the pinned input (#776's ledger
  replays 128's gate to 2.4e-6 in) — it enters upstream of the gate, and it is a
  root-cause item for #776, not a tolerance item.

## Verdict: (a)

The pin's 1e-9 inch is below the instrument's own float32 noise. Seven of the
ten flags (003 006 028 033 109 110 119) are float noise, at most 0.38 ULP of the
board's inch scale over the pin, and #776 does not change geometry on any of
them. The three that remain (026 037 128) are geometric — 72 ULP at the
smallest — and stay red under the new rule; their owners are named above.
(b) is false for 003/109/110/119: the line that moves them is the mirror write
`cfg[i].c = [s.c[0] as f64 / IN2M + board_in[0] * 0.5, …]` in `overlap_pass`,
by design one narrowing instead of two, and the move is ≤ 0.5 ULP.

## The harness change (`fix(tools): position_parity — ULP-aware accepted delta`)

Rule: the regression gate allows `ULP_SLACK = 4` float32 ULPs of the board's
inch scale on top of a pinned accepted delta,
`slack_in(board) = 4 * ulp32(max(board_in))` = 3.05e-5 in on a 72 in board
(scaling with the board), and a model pinned bit-equal that lands inside the
slack keeps its bucket. The summary line's `equal` keeps its 1e-9 in meaning —
bit-equality is still reported, it is just not a ratchet. Why four: the
float-noise class measured on 304 positions tops out at 0.38 ULP over the pin
(0.62 ULP endpoint move); the smallest geometric excess is 72 ULP; four is an
order of magnitude from both and 6,500 times below a 0.2 in move.

- GREEN — the patched harness on the same reports: main `REGRESSION` 026 037

## Hygiene item for the farm scripts (private, not edited here)

`box_test.sh parity` writes the harness output to a per-slot directory
(`parity_out_<slot>`) and the next job in that slot deletes it. This morning's
main and #776 outputs were gone before anyone read a per-model delta; both had
to be re-run (two box hours). The runner should keep the harness output per job
(`/root/work/jobs/<jid>/parity_out/`, next to `out`, `rc`, `slot`) and copy
`parity_<slot>.log` beside it, so a verdict's per-model numbers survive the
next dispatch and a reader can re-check a `REGRESSION` line with
`--check-report` instead of re-running Godot.
